# Research and Fact-Checking

## When research is needed
Research claims that are current, disputed, technical, scientific, historical, legal, medical, financial, or otherwise consequential. For stable elementary concepts, use authoritative foundational knowledge and cite sources if the user requests them.

## Source priority
1. Primary research, official datasets, standards, laws, and official documentation.
2. Peer-reviewed reviews and academic textbooks.
3. Government, university, and recognized institutional publications.
4. Reputable specialist publications.
5. Secondary explainers only as supporting context.

## Research ledger
For each source record:
- Claim supported
- Author or organization
- Title
- Publication/update date
- URL or bibliographic details
- Access date when relevant
- Limitations or conflicts

## Editorial rules
- Verify central claims independently when feasible.
- Do not invent citations, dates, statistics, quotations, or attributions.
- Distinguish correlation from causation.
- Identify uncertainty and disagreement.
- For changing facts, state the relevant date.
- Use original wording; do not copy source passages.
- Use short quotations only when necessary and respect copyright limits.
- Mark illustrative examples as illustrative; do not present them as real cases.
- Do not create fake source screenshots, fake citations, or fake data visualizations.
